## Overview

We designed and simulated a nozzle that would provide specific values of thrust while attached to an existing combustion chamber / nozzle adapter assembly. This was a project to introduce new members to the Boston University Rocket Propulsion Group. 

## My approach

Before designing the rocket nozzle we had to learn what we were building and how it worked. After reading up the first 3 chapters of Rocket Propulsion Elements (9th edition) to find specific equations on mass flow rate and thrust we were able to identify the key values listed in the table below.

## Results

We then designed the ideal bell nozzle in Solidworks. 

One challenge we faced was determining how much material should remain on the outside of the rocket nozzle. Theoretically, the shape of the nozzle shown below did not have to be cone shaped. The outer shape of the nozzle could look like a cylinder as long as the inside values like throat and exit diameter were the same as calculated.   


We decided that it would be fun to cut down on the outer thickness and test if the nozzle was still safe to use. Some our results are shown below. They show that the nozzle would survive our applied pressures.

## What I learned

This project taught and exposed me to Solidworks simulation. The project was also cool in that we had to learn theory about the project on our own. Reading up on Rocket theory, learning about terminologies such as mass flow rate and thrust, connecting it back to the values we had to find, determining our values and sharing it with more experienced engineers, going to the drawing board and figuring out what kind of nozzle to use, designing the nozzle, and simulating it was a really fun experience that gave us a lot of independence and introduced us to a lot.
